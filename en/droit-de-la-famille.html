  • Life insurance contract

In principle, life insurance is not part of the estate. It therefore escapes the rules protecting forced heirs, such as the reduction of excessive gifts and bequests, as well as the provisions on the collation of gifts, which aim to guarantee equality between heirs.

However, the Insurance Code provides that manifestly excessive premiums paid into a life insurance contract may be brought back into the estate. The issue is to assess the proportionality between the amounts paid into the contract and the deceased's income or assets.

The Furlan & Associés firm supports you throughout the negotiations regarding this assessment. If no amicable solution can settle the dispute, we will guide you towards the best judicial route to defend your interests.

Cross-border divorce

We understand the complexities of local disputes with cross-border features and we support you at every stage of the procedure.

The firm has particular expertise in the division of the Swiss second pillar and in the splitting of the AVS pension (1st pillar).

The division of occupational pension provision is indeed one of the ancillary effects of divorce in Switzerland that must necessarily be taken into account.

The firm also masters the question of EPL advances, that is, the case of a loan from the occupational pension fund to the spouse, which occurs in particular when the marital home is purchased.

This question indeed arises regularly.

When this sale takes place within divorce proceedings, it is essential to legally characterise this advance and to take it into account in the accounting, liquidation and division of the spouses' matrimonial property regime.

Cross-border succession

The FURLAN and associates firm is competent in international successions and supports you in particular in the settlement of cross-border successions.

To do so, we apply the international instruments, in particular European Regulation No 650/2012 and the Swiss Federal Act on Private International Law (PILA).

We support you at every stage of the settlement of a cross-border succession:

  • Determination of the legal heirs, forced heirs and appointed heirs and their shares (system of the three parentelas),
  • Rights of the surviving spouse (who, since 1 January 2023, loses the status of forced heir if divorce proceedings are pending at the time of death and if the spouses agreed on the principle of divorce or had already lived apart for at least two years),
  • Determination of the estate to be divided and of the mass used to calculate the reserved shares,
  • Partition,
  • Swiss succession planning instruments,
  • Litigation in Swiss succession law.
